What is a Fraction?

A fraction represents a part of a whole. It has two components:

  • Numerator (top number) - how many parts you have
  • Denominator (bottom number) - how many parts make up the whole

Visual Representation:

3 — 4

means "3 parts out of 4 total parts"

Operations with Fractions

Addition & Subtraction

To add or subtract fractions, they must have the same denominator (common denominator).

1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4    (same denominator: just add numerators)

1/3 + 1/4 = ?
Step 1: Find common denominator (12)
Step 2: Convert: 4/12 + 3/12
Step 3: Add: 7/12

Multiplication

Multiply numerators together, multiply denominators together.

2/3 × 3/4 = (2×3)/(3×4) = 6/12 = 1/2

Division

Flip the second fraction (reciprocal) and multiply.

2/3 ÷ 3/4 = 2/3 × 4/3 = 8/9

What are Exponents?

An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.

Notation:

23 = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

Read as "2 to the power of 3" or "2 cubed"

  • Base: the number being multiplied (2)
  • Exponent: how many times to multiply (3)

Special Cases:

  • Any number to the power of 0 equals 1: x⁰ = 1
  • Any number to the power of 1 equals itself: x¹ = x
  • Negative exponents mean reciprocal: x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ

Exponent Rules

Product Rule

xᵃ × xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ

2³ × 2² = 2⁵ = 32

Quotient Rule

xᵃ ÷ xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ

2⁵ ÷ 2² = 2³ = 8

Power Rule

(xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃˣᵇ

(2²)³ = 2⁶ = 64

Product to Power

(xy)ᵃ = xᵃyᵃ

(2×3)² = 2²×3² = 36

Quotient to Power

(x/y)ᵃ = xᵃ/yᵃ

(4/2)² = 4²/2² = 4

Negative Exponent

x⁻ᵃ = 1/xᵃ

2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8

Logarithms: The Inverse of Exponents

A logarithm answers the question: "What power do I need to raise the base to, to get this number?"

Relationship between Exponents and Logs:

2³ = 8 ⟺ log₂(8) = 3

"2 to what power equals 8?" Answer: 3

Common Logarithms:

  • log₁₀ (or just "log") - base 10, common in science
  • ln - natural log (base e ≈ 2.718), used in calculus
  • log₂ - base 2, used in computer science

Statistics is about understanding uncertainty and finding patterns in data.

What is Probability?

Probability measures how likely an event is to occur, expressed as a number between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).

Basic Formula:

Probability = (Number of favorable outcomes) / (Total number of possible outcomes)

Example: Coin Flip

Probability of heads = 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%

  • Favorable outcomes: 1 (heads)
  • Total outcomes: 2 (heads or tails)

Descriptive Statistics

Statistics help us summarize and understand data. Key measures include:

Mean (Average)

Mean = Sum of all values / Number of values

Example: [2, 4, 6, 8, 10] → Mean = 30/5 = 6

Median (Middle Value)

The middle value when data is sorted

Example: [2, 4, 6, 8, 10] → Median = 6

Mode (Most Common)

The value that appears most frequently

Example: [1, 2, 2, 3, 4] → Mode = 2

Range

Range = Maximum value - Minimum value

Example: [2, 4, 6, 8, 10] → Range = 10 - 2 = 8

Normal Distribution (Bell Curve)

Many natural phenomena follow a normal distribution - most values cluster around the mean, with fewer extreme values.

68-95-99.7 Rule:

  • 68% of data falls within 1 standard deviation of the mean
  • 95% falls within 2 standard deviations
  • 99.7% falls within 3 standard deviations
